# Founders Inflections

Keep prospective founders informed about technology changes from which they can form their own founder insights. Report developments and factual context; do not invent startup ideas, opportunity theses, or recommendations unless requested.

## Research and selection

Use live web research for every issue. Cover the preceding seven days in America/Indiana/Indianapolis time, state the coverage dates and research cutoff, and distinguish publication date from event date. Read prior issues in content/ to avoid recycling stories; include follow-ups only when something material changed. After a missed run, flag any coverage gap and separately label significant catch-up items.

Search broadly across AI and models, developer tools, cloud and compute, data infrastructure, cybersecurity, APIs and interoperability, workflow automation, and horizontal and vertical SaaS. Include hardware or research advances when their connection to software capabilities is concrete. Avoid letting AI headlines crowd out other relevant developments.

Prioritize changes in what can be built, performance, cost, access, licensing, integration, distribution, or adoption. Favor releases, new APIs, open-source projects, credible research results, pricing changes, and platform shifts. Exclude routine funding, executive news, rumors, and promotional launches without a meaningful technology change. Do not present every release as a proven inflection.

Use news outlets and public feeds for discovery, then open original announcements, documentation, release notes, repositories, or papers. Support technical claims with primary sources. Use independent reporting for additional context where useful. Verify dates and material numerical claims; distinguish vendor benchmarks from independent evidence and demos or previews from generally available products. If full text is unavailable, find accessible corroboration or omit unsupported details. Do not reproduce articles or bypass access restrictions.

## Issue format

Aim for a useful 8–12 minute read, normally 8–12 strong developments, with fewer when the evidence is thin. Prefer substance over a quota.

Every issue must include one or two technology stories that a reader without a technical background can understand. Choose substantive developments in everyday products, business workflows, customer experiences, or costs, with concrete before-and-after examples. Explain what a person or business can do differently, define essential terms, and omit unnecessary acronyms and implementation details. Include these within the normal story count. Keep the facts and limitations intact; accessible stories need the same source verification as technical stories. Do not turn them into startup ideas. Across the rest of the issue, explain unfamiliar terms on first use.

Start with the title Founders Inflections, issue date, coverage window, and a short factual overview. For each development include:
- A clear headline and category.
- What changed, including the prior state where verified.
- The affected capability, workflow, or constraint, grounded in the evidence.
- Availability and limitations such as preview status, access, licensing, pricing, or unvalidated performance claims.
- Event date and clickable descriptive source links, with publisher and publication date.

Use concise plain English, explain unfamiliar terms, and avoid hype. Optional brief follow-ups may track previously covered releases whose availability or evidence changed. Do not add speculative founder insights.
